# Cycle Normalization

> **Operator supplement.** Apply only when the Industry DNA / Economic Archetype indicates material cyclicality. It does not override module-specific terminal policy or DCF–PER consistency gates.

Cyclical companies should not capitalize a temporary peak or trough as a perpetual state.

## Establish the cycle state first

Use evidence such as price/spread, volume, inventory, utilization, order/backlog, lead time, capacity additions, replacement cycle and customer CAPEX. A calendar-year average by itself is not a cycle model.

## Valuation treatment

- **Explicit forecast:** may reflect the current cycle when supported by evidence.
- **Convergence period:** normalize price, utilization, margin, reinvestment and returns toward an economically sustainable state.
- **Terminal state:** use through-cycle economics consistent with terminal growth and terminal ROIC. Do not normalize margin while leaving peak utilization, peak pricing or sub-normal reinvestment unchanged.
- **Exit multiple:** may be a cross-check when the archetype permits it, but do not average it mechanically with a Gordon result. Reconcile why the methods differ.

A 3–5 year historical average can be a reference, not an automatic answer. Structural changes in technology, capacity discipline, regulation, mix or industry concentration can invalidate the old average.

## Cross-method gate

Core Fundamental PER must use the same normalized growth, margin, reinvestment and duration worldview as the Core DCF. A “mid-cycle DCF” paired with a peak-cycle EPS multiple is inconsistent and should fail the DCF–PER assumption consistency gate.
