Letter from the Executive Board 
Ladies and gentlemen,  
dear shareholders, 
In the first half of 2026, the Lufthansa Group’s business performance was shaped by a 
challenging market environment due to the crisis in the Middle East. While the higher 
kerosene price and temporary route cancellations adversely affected the Group’s operating 
and financial performance, strong demand on its Asian and African routes had a positive 
impact.  
Overall, our Network and Point-to-Point Airlines reliably carried more than 60 million 
passengers to their destinations, thanks to higher load factors. However, the earnings 
trend was negative in both of these business segments in the first half of the year due to 
the effects of the Iran war, as well as strikes. 
The Logistics and MRO business segments continued to develop successfully in the first 
half of 2026 and once again achieved positive earnings figures that were higher than in the 
previous year. Lufthansa Cargo in particular benefited from changed freight routes and 
logistics flows as a result of geopolitical upheavals. 
Adjusted EBIT for the Lufthansa Group came to EUR -229m in the first half of 2026, which 
represents a EUR 378m decrease year-on-year. However, in the second quarter a positive 
Adjusted EBIT of EUR 383m was achieved. For the year as a whole, the Lufthansa Group 
expects an Adjusted EBIT of between EUR 1.7 and 2.2bn. The further course of the Middle 
East conflict and its effects on kerosene prices in particular are key factors shaping the 
outlook. 
Consistent implementation of our strategy will enable us to overcome the volatility facing 
our industry and the impacts of geopolitical crises. In response to the strong rise in 
kerosene prices, we have decided to move forward with a number of measures to 
streamline our operational processes more rapidly than originally planned. These include 
the removal of Lufthansa CityLine’s flight operations from the service offering, the 
associated decommissioning of the entire Canadair CR-9 sub-fleet of 23 aircraft as well as 
the early retirement of fuel-intensive long-haul aircraft such as the Airbus A340-600 and 
the temporary grounding of two Boeing 747-400s from the beginning of the winter flight 
schedule. This will reduce fuel consumption as well as the impact on earnings of the non-
hedged portion of the Group’s fuel. We are also reducing the complexity of our fleet and 
thus improving our competitiveness.  
Moreover, the continuing implementation of Lufthansa Airlines’ turnaround programme  
is having a substantial positive impact on earnings and operational stability. Our 
restructuring of the Lufthansa Group is also proceeding according to schedule. Lufthansa 
City Airlines’ fleet is continuously growing, its fleet modernisation and cabin refit measures 
are progressing and the updated Future Onboard Experience (FOX) on long-haul flights has 
been successfully introduced across all travel classes at Lufthansa Airlines. We are thus 
improving the first-class premium experience for our passengers and setting new 
standards in the area of customer satisfaction. 
Our integration of ITA Airways is also rapidly progressing. In June, we exercised our option 
to acquire a majority stake in the Italian airline as planned. Our equity investment will thus 
increase from 41 to 90%. Following the completion of the transaction at the start of next 
year, ITA Airways will also be fully integrated within the Lufthansa Group in organisational 
terms and financially consolidated. 
Together, we intend to take the Lufthansa Group forward into a successful future as an 
integrated airline group with our four central business segments Network Airlines, Point-
to-Point Airlines, Logistics and MRO. For the benefit of our shareholders, customers, 
passengers and employees. 
We are pleased that you are accompanying us on our journey. 
Frankfurt, 31 July 2026 
 
 
Carsten Spohr, Chairman of the Executive Board

The average price of oil (Brent ICE) climbed by 24% year-
on-year in the first half of 2026 to USD 87.44/barrel 
(previous year: USD 70.75/bbl). 
The jet fuel crack, the price difference between crude oil 
and kerosene, averaged USD 56.92/barrel in the first half of 
2026 and is thus up by 201% year-on-year (previous year: 
USD 18.94/barrel). 
Accordingly, the average kerosene price likewise increased 
significantly by 60% year-on-year to USD 1,133.90/t 
(previous year: USD 707.86/t). 
The euro generally performed positively on average against 
the major currencies in the first half of 2026. The Japanese 
yen registered the strongest change, with a decline of 14% 
against the euro. The euro gained 7% against the US dollar 
and 4% against the Canadian dollar. The British pound 
suffered an average fall of 3% against the euro. The euro 
rose by 1% against the Chinese renminbi. On the other 
hand, the euro dropped 2% against the Swiss franc. 
The conflict in the Middle East pushed up the global rate of 
inflation due to high oil prices. According to Bloomberg, 
inflation stood at 4.4% in June 2026. In Europe, too, the 
increased energy costs were reflected in a higher rate of 
inflation, which reached 2.8% in the Eurozone and 2.3% in 
Germany. In this context, in June 2026 the European 
Central Bank raised its key interest rate to 2.4% in order to 
counter the rising inflation risks. The US key interest rate 
remained unchanged at 3.75% in the first half of 2026.

In the first five months of the reporting year for which 
estimates from the International Air Transport Association 
(IATA) are currently available, the sales trend for the global 
passenger business improved only slightly on the previous 
year. According to IATA, the number of passenger-
kilometres sold worldwide rose by 1% year-on-year in the 
first five months of 2026. The trend was dampened by the 
conflict in the Middle East in particular, where sales 
declined by 25%. In Europe, the volume of sales rose  
by 4%. 
Since the start of the conflict in the Middle East, the 
significantly higher kerosene prices have resulted in a 
considerable change in conditions for the aviation sector. 
The airlines have responded to this by moderately reducing 
their capacity as well as raising ticket prices to pass on at 
least part of the increased cost burden to their customers. 
Growth in passenger numbers has thus slowed, while the 
underlying demand for travel remains robust. 
At the same time, there has been a clear shift in global 
passenger flows. Demand for flights to the Middle East 
(including connections via the region’s hubs) has dropped 
significantly due to the operational restrictions, while 
alternative traffic flows to Asia and Europe especially have 
gained in significance. 
In the medium term, capacity growth in the industry is 
expected to remain limited due to continuing aircraft and 
engine supply bottlenecks. Limited capacity contrasts with 
a continued high level of demand and is thus, on the whole, 
bolstering the yield trend. 
The global market for airfreight also grew in the first five 
months of the 2026 financial year, supported by the 
continuing boom in online trade. At the same time, 
geopolitical upheavals such as the crisis in the Middle East 
and closed maritime transport corridors are having a 
positive impact on airfreight volumes and yields. According 
to IATA, global airfreight volumes (measured in revenue 
cargo tonne-kilometres) climbed by 4% year-on-year. In 
Europe, the volume of sales rose by 6%. By contrast, the 
airfreight volume in the Middle East has fallen by 13% due 
to the ongoing conflict. 
The aircraft maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) 
business once again developed positively overall in the first 
half of 2026. On the whole, a continued high level of 
demand for flights supported demand for MRO services. 
However, in the second quarter of 2026, the Iran crisis and 
the related temporary decrease in flight hours led to a 
reduced level of demand in some service areas. Moreover, 
the cost situation among MRO providers was negatively 
impacted by the ongoing shortage of materials due to 
delays in deliveries from manufacturers and suppliers of 
aircraft, engines and aircraft components as well as 
additional tariffs, including on aluminium and steel 
products.

Lufthansa Group’s earnings below previous year’s level  
In the first half of 2026, the business performance of the 
Lufthansa Group’s passenger business was substantially 
impacted by the effects of the crisis in the Middle East. 
While the higher kerosene price and the temporary route 
cancellations adversely affected operating and financial 
performance, strong demand on its Asian and African 
routes had a positive impact on traffic. Various strikes 
staged by the Vereinigung Cockpit pilots’ union (VC) and 
the Unabhängige Flugbegleiter Organisation union for 
cabin crew (UFO) also had a negative impact.  
Capacity in the Lufthansa Group’s passenger business 
declined by 2% year-on-year in the first half of 2026.  
On the other hand, the passenger load factor went up by 
1.9 percentage points to 82.4%. 
The Network Airlines’ Adjusted EBIT decreased to EUR 
-468m in the first half of 2026 (previous year: EUR -113m).
In the Point-to-Point Airlines business segment, Adjusted
EBIT fell to EUR -252m (previous year: EUR -137m).
In the Logistics business segment, the positive operating 
and financial trend continued in the first half of 2026. As a 
result, Lufthansa Cargo expanded its volume of traffic and 
achieved an Adjusted EBIT of EUR 199m, which was 47% 
higher than in the previous year (previous year: EUR 135m). 
In the MRO business segment, a continued high level of 
demand for flights strengthened demand for maintenance 
and repair services. The Middle East conflict, the weaker 
US dollar, inflation- and growth-related cost increases and 
punitive US tariffs had a negative effect. In the first half of 
2026, Adjusted EBIT at Lufthansa Technik rose by 2% to 
EUR 315m (previous year: EUR 310m).  
↗ Business segments. 
The Lufthansa Group’s revenue increased by 8% year-on-
year to EUR 19,887m (previous year: EUR 18,449m) due to 
higher passenger traffic yields as well as strong growth in 
its Logistics and MRO business segments. 
The Lufthansa Group’s operating expenses rose by 7% to 
EUR 21,390m (previous year: EUR 19,963m), above all due 
to a significant increase in fuel expenses. 
The Lufthansa Group’s Adjusted EBIT came to EUR -229m 
in the first half of 2026 and was thus lower than in the 
previous year (previous year: EUR 149m).  
The Adjusted EBIT margin fell by 2.0 percentage points to 
-1.1% (previous year: 0.8%). ↗ Earnings position.
Free cash flow roughly on par with previous year 
The Lufthansa Group’s cash flow from operating activities 
fell by 20% in the first half of 2026 to EUR 2,264m 
(previous year: EUR 2,831m). This decline is primarily due to 
lower Adjusted EBITDA as well as reduced cash inflows 
from working capital. 
Adjusted free cash flow amounted to EUR 1,015m and was 
thus roughly at previous year’s level (previous year: 
EUR 1,024m). The decrease in cash flow from operating 
activities was compensated for by the decline in net capital 
expenditure.  
As of 30 June 2026, the Lufthansa Group’s overall available 
liquidity amounted to EUR 10,727m (31 December 2025: 
EUR 10,715m). ↗ Financial position. 
Increase in the Lufthansa Group’s indebtedness 
As of 30 June 2026, the Lufthansa Group’s net 
indebtedness amounted to EUR 6,697m; EUR 290m higher 
than at year-end 2025 (31 December 2025: EUR 6,407m).  
Net pension obligations were down compared to the end of 
2025 and came to EUR 1,585m (31 December 2025: 
EUR 1,902m), above all due to positive valuation effects for 
the plan assets.  
The Adjusted net debt/Adjusted EBITDA ratio in the past 
twelve months stood at 2.0 as of 30 June 2026 and was 
thus higher than at the end of 2025  
(31 December 2025: 1.8). ↗ Net assets. 
Significant events 
VC and UFO trade unions call for strikes 
Following several strikes in the first quarter, the trade 
unions VC and UFO called for further strikes in April 2026. 
VC called for strikes at Lufthansa Classic, Lufthansa Cargo 
and Lufthansa CityLine on four days in April and at 
Eurowings on two days in April. 
UFO called for strikes at Lufthansa Classic and Lufthansa 
CityLine on three days in April, partly coinciding with VC’s 
strike action. 
These strikes caused significant temporary disruptions to 
flight operations. 
With these strikes, VC is seeking to secure improved 
retirement benefits for pilots at Lufthansa Classic and 
Lufthansa Cargo. The collective bargaining dispute with 
UFO relates to the conclusion of a new framework 
agreement with Lufthansa Classic.

Lufthansa City Airlines and ver.di reach collective 
agreement for flight staff 
On 10 April 2026, Lufthansa City Airlines and the trade 
union Vereinigte Dienstleistungsgewerkschaft (ver.di) 
reached an agreement in principle on a comprehensive 
wage settlement framework for flight staff. This agreement 
has a minimum term of three years and sets out clear 
conditions for cabin and cockpit personnel. 
Lufthansa Group accelerates implementation of strategy 
and removes CityLine from its programme 
On 16 April 2026, the Lufthansa Group announced that it 
would bring forward the implementation of some aspects 
of its corporate strategy on account of a significant rise in 
kerosene prices as well as growing financial and operational 
pressures as a result of industrial action. In this context, it 
resolved an initial package involving a reduction of its flight 
programme on short-, medium- and long-haul routes as 
well as measures to bring forward the modernisation of its 
fleet. 
As a first step which had an immediate impact, Lufthansa 
CityLine’s 27 aircraft still in operation on 16 April 2026 were 
removed from the service offering. Overall, all 39 of 
Lufthansa CityLine’s aircraft were removed from the 
service offering, with six A319s and its four A321-P2Fs 
being redeployed for other flight operations. 
Moreover, following the end of its summer flight timetable, 
Lufthansa Airlines intends to cut its long-haul capacity by a 
total of four Airbus A340-600 intercontinental aircraft. 
From October, it will also implement an initial temporary 
reduction of two Boeing 747-400s over the coming winter. 
In addition, in its coming winter flight timetable, it will 
reduce capacities for short- and medium-haul flights. 
This package of measures will have a disproportionate 
impact in terms of fuel cost savings. On the one hand, 
particularly inefficient aircraft will be removed from the 
network ahead of schedule. On the other, the volume of 
kerosene saved will reduce the non-hedged portion of the 
Group’s fuel requirements. 
Lufthansa Group orders ten Airbus and ten Boeing long-
haul aircraft 
At its meeting on 11 May 2026, the Supervisory Board of 
Deutsche Lufthansa AG approved orders for a total of 20 
new long-haul aircraft. The Executive Board had previously 
decided to order ten Airbus A350-900s and ten Boeing 
787-9s. These aircraft are due to be delivered between 
2032 and 2034.  
Shareholders approve all Annual General Meeting agenda 
items 
Deutsche Lufthansa AG’s 73rd Annual General Meeting 
took place on 12 May 2026. Its shareholders approved all of 
the items on the agenda by large majorities. 
The agenda items at the Annual General Meeting included 
the use of distributable earnings, with the distribution of a 
dividend of EUR 0.33 per share envisaged, as well as the 
election of members of the Supervisory Board of 
Deutsche Lufthansa AG. Dr Johannes Teyssen, former CEO 
of E.ON SE, and Wolfgang Nickl, former Member of the 
Board of Management and CFO of Bayer AG, have been 
elected to the Supervisory Board for the first time. Karl 
Gernandt, President of the Board of Directors of Kühne 
Holding AG, was re-elected to the Supervisory Board. 
Following the Annual General Meeting, the Supervisory 
Board elected Dr Johannes Teyssen as its chairman. 
Deutsche Lufthansa AG exercises option to acquire 
majority stake in ITA Airways 
In June 2026, Deutsche Lufthansa AG exercised its option 
to acquire a majority stake in ITA Airways. Its equity 
investment will thus increase from 41 to 90%.  
Deutsche Lufthansa AG is to acquire this further 49% share 
tranche at an agreed purchase price of EUR 325m. The 
transaction is expected to be completed in the first quarter 
of 2027, subject to official approval from the European 
Commission and the US Department of Justice (DOJ) in 
particular. ITA Airways will subsequently be fully integrated 
within the Lufthansa Group in organisational and financial 
terms. 
Events after the reporting 
date 
On 29 July 2026 the Lufthansa Group submitted a bid to 
Parpública, the Portuguese state-owned investment 
company, as part of the bidding process for TAP Air 
Portugal. The acquisition of an initial minority stake is 
intended to establish a long-term partnership between the 
Lufthansa Group and TAP Air Portugal in order to secure 
TAP’s successful future as Portugal’s national airline on a 
sustainable basis. 
The partnership would strengthen Lisbon’s position as a 
strategically important Atlantic hub within the Lufthansa 
Group’s network. Connectivity between Europe and other 
regions, such as Latin America, Africa, and North America, 
could be expanded.

Traffic revenue for Lufthansa Group airlines up 5%  
year-on-year 
The Lufthansa Group reduced its passenger business 
capacity (available seat-kilometres) by 2% year-on-year in 
the first half of 2026. Sales (revenue seat-kilometres) were 
at the same level as in the previous year. The passenger 
load factor was up by 1.9 percentage points at 82.4%. 
Traffic revenue in the passenger business climbed by 3% to 
EUR 13,659m (previous year: EUR 13,199m). This increase 
primarily reflects higher yields. The crisis in the Middle East 
– due to the temporary cancellation of individual routes – 
and the strikes in February, March and April 2026 had a 
negative impact. 
In the Lufthansa Group’s cargo business, capacity 
(available cargo tonne-kilometres) was 4% higher than in 
the previous year due to increased belly capacities.  
Sales (revenue cargo tonne-kilometres) likewise grew by 
4% in comparison with the previous year. The cargo load 
factor decreased by 0.1 percentage points to 58.5%. Traffic 
revenue in the cargo business rose by 16% to EUR 2,089m 
due to higher yields and stronger sales (previous year: 
EUR 1,808m). 
Compared with the previous year, traffic revenue at 
Lufthansa Group airlines rose overall by 5% in the first half 
of 2026 to EUR 15,748m (previous year: EUR 15,009m).  
 
Revenue up 8% year-on-year 
Other revenue rose by 20% to EUR 4,139m (previous year: 
EUR 3,440m), mainly due to the increase in business 
activities and the associated higher volume of revenue in 
the MRO business segment. 
Revenue, which consists of traffic revenue plus other 
revenue, increased by 8% in the first half of 2026 to 
EUR 19,887m (previous year: EUR 18,449m).  
Due to lower foreign exchange gains in particular, other 
operating income fell by 14% to EUR 1,343m (previous year: 
EUR 1,567m).  
Operating income increased by 6% overall to EUR 21,230m 
(previous year: EUR 20,016m).  
Operating expenses up 7% year-on-year 
Operating expenses at the Lufthansa Group rose by 7% 
year-on-year in the first half of 2026 to EUR 21,390m 
(previous year: EUR 19,963m).  
The cost of materials and services at the Lufthansa Group 
came to EUR 12,594m, an increase of 10% on the previous 
year (previous year: EUR 11,402m). 
Expenses for fuel rose overall by 18% to EUR 4,176m 
(previous year: EUR 3,536m). The effects of the fuel price

Gross capital expenditure of EUR 2.1bn 
In the first half of 2026, the Lufthansa Group’s gross capital 
expenditure (without acquisition of equity investments) 
amounted to EUR 2,142m and was thus 31% higher than in 
the previous year (previous year: EUR 1,637m). This mainly 
related to final payments for 14 aircraft received (five 
Airbus A320s, including one lease buyout; one A321; one 
A350; and seven Boeing 787-9s), capitalised major 
maintenance events and advance payments for future 
aircraft purchases. 
Net capital expenditure (without acquisition and disposal of 
equity investments) amounted to EUR 994m and was thus 
38% lower than in the previous year (previous year: 
EUR 1,608m). This figure includes payments for aircraft 
spare parts as well as dividend and interest income. The 
decrease mainly resulted from cash inflows arising from 
sales of property, plant and equipment in the amount of 
EUR 1,062m (previous year: EUR 22m), in particular sales of 
aircraft in the context of sale and leaseback transactions. 
EUR 2.3bn generated in cash flow from operating 
activities 
The Lufthansa Group achieved a cash flow from operating 
activities of EUR 2,264m in the first half of 2026. This was 
20% lower than in the previous year (previous year: 
EUR 2,831m). This decline is primarily due to lower 
Adjusted EBITDA as well as reduced cash inflows from 
working capital.  
The cash inflow from the change in trade working capital 
amounted to EUR 1,402m in the first half of 2026 (previous 
year: EUR 1,756m). This was associated with increased 
liabilities from unused flight documents, which rose by 
EUR 1,802m in the first half of 2026 (previous year: 
EUR 2,223m). Effects from reduced customer receivables 
and contract assets/liabilities came to EUR -792m 
(previous year: EUR -387m); higher supplier liabilities 
amounted to EUR 983m (previous year: EUR 208m). 
Effects of EUR -467m (previous year: EUR -78m) arose 
from the increase in other receivables and advance 
payments, in particular advance payments to suppliers.  
In addition, the change in other assets, liabilities and 
provisions – in particular, in connection with accrued 
employee obligations – gave rise to effects in the amount 
of EUR -146m (previous year: EUR -139m).  
 
Adjusted free cash flow at EUR 1.0bn

EUR 1,015m and was thus at roughly the same level as in 
the previous year (previous year: EUR 1,024m). The 
decrease in cash flow from operating activities was 
compensated for by the decline in net capital expenditure 
(without acquisition and disposal of equity investments) 
due to incoming payments from aircraft sales.

Non-current assets decrease by EUR 257m 
As of 30 June 2026, non-current assets of EUR 31,979m 
were EUR 257m lower than at year-end 2025

In particular, deferred tax assets (EUR -224m), investments 
accounted for using the equity method (EUR -122m), and 
aircraft and reserve engines (EUR -65m) each decreased. 
This was partially offset by an increase in other property, 
plant and equipment in particular (EUR +121m). 
The decline in deferred tax assets related to the tax effects 
arising from the increase in the market values of financial 
derivatives in particular. 
The decrease in equity investments mainly arose due to the 
negative earnings contributions provided by SunExpress 
and ITA Airways.  
The decrease in aircraft and reserve engines is based on 
increased depreciation and disposals, which were largely 
made up for by capital expenditure on 14 new aircraft, 
major maintenance events, advance payments on existing

value of aircraft and reserve engines amounted to 
EUR 19,755m as of 30 June 2026 (31 December 2025: 
EUR 19,820m). The Lufthansa Group fleet comprised a total 
of 740 aircraft (31 December 2025: 737 aircraft).

related to capital expenditure on the modernisation of 
Lufthansa Cargo’s freight centre in Frankfurt, the 
construction of a Lufthansa Technik production facility in 
Portugal and lounges. 
Current assets increase by EUR 2.7bn 
As of 30 June 2026, current assets were up EUR 2,737m at 
EUR 18,895m (31 December 2025: EUR 16,158m). 
Trade receivables and other receivables (EUR +1,145m), 
derivative financial instruments (EUR +715m), cash and 
cash equivalents (EUR +639m) and assets held for sale 
(EUR +570m) in particular increased. This was offset by a 
decline in interest-bearing securities and similar 
investments (EUR -643m). 
The increase in trade receivables and other receivables is 
mainly based on the seasonal growth in the passenger 
business and increased revenue in the MRO segment, 
advance payments made to suppliers as well as higher 
claims arising from plan asset surpluses for pension plans. 
The increase in derivative financial instruments mainly 
relates to changes in the market values of fuel and currency 
hedges. 
The growth in assets held for sale resulted from the 
reclassification of a total of 34 aircraft which are to be 
included in sale and leaseback transactions (in particular, 
nine Boeing 787-9s) within the next twelve months or else 
retired (above all, five Airbus A340-300s). This was offset 
by disposals of ten aircraft through sales, of which seven 
were sold through sale and leaseback transactions. 
The decrease in interest-bearing securities and similar 
investments is primarily based on the reduced investment 
of bank balances in fixed-term deposits with a term of up 
to one year. 
Non-current provisions and liabilities almost unchanged

30 June 2026 (31 December 2025: EUR 1,902m).

Lufthansa Technik once again reported a positive course of 
business in the first half of 2026. A continued high level of 
demand for flights supported demand for maintenance and 
repair services as well as other products and services from 
Lufthansa Technik.  
However, in the second quarter of 2026, the crisis in the 
Middle East adversely affected operating performance due 
to reduced flight hours as well as a drop in demand for 
certain MRO services. To limit further earnings pressure, 
cost-cutting measures were initiated, projects were 
prioritized more strongly and the filling of vacancies was 
limited. 
Moreover, the weakening of the US dollar had a negative 
impact year-on-year. The sustainably elevated costs 
resulting from US tariffs are being passed on to customers 
wherever contractually and commercially possible. 
The ongoing shortage of materials on the global market 
also continues to weigh on business development, driven 
by delays in deliveries by the manufacturers and suppliers 
of aircraft, engines and aircraft components.  
Despite these operational and geopolitical challenges, 
Lufthansa Technik achieved further commercial success in 
the second quarter of 2026. Lufthansa Technik signed a 
contract with Air India covering technical services for 
Honeywell HGT1700 auxiliary power units (APUs) for its 
A350 fleet. Lufthansa Technik will thus strengthen its 
position in relation to maintenance of this APU type. 
It has also achieved another milestone in the Defence 
business. Under the contract signed with Boeing in the 
previous year, the German Navy for the first time handed 
over Boeing P-8A Poseidon aircraft to Lufthansa Technik 
Defense in Hamburg for scheduled maintenance. As well as 
aircraft checks, this contract covers the supply of 
components, monitoring the condition of engines and 
operational management. 
At the same time, Lufthansa Technik is moving forward 
with the strategic expansion projects planned as part of its 
Ambition 2030 programme. June 2026 the construction of 
Lufthansa Technik Portugal’s approximately 55,000 square 
metre production facility began. Moreover, Lufthansa 
Technik Canada has successfully completed its first engine 
maintenance work at its interim facility.  
In addition, Lufthansa Technik Philippines has announced 
that it intends to build a second aircraft overhaul location 
covering more than 157,000 square metres at Clark 
International Airport.

Opportunities and risk report 
The opportunities and risks for the Group described in 
detail in the Annual Report 2025 have materialised or 
developed as follows: 
The safety and operational situation in the Middle East 
remains volatile and may impact the Lufthansa Group’s 
flight operations and operational planning. In this 
context, the Group has implemented operational 
measures including cancellations of flights to affected 
areas and rerouting around the corresponding airspace. 
Since conditions are continuously shifting, there will be 
an ongoing need to proactively monitor the situation 
over the coming months. At the present time, there is 
only limited predictability regarding medium- to long-
term effects on flight operations and operational 
stability. 
The conflict in the Middle East and the continued 
disruption to global supply and transport chains may 
significantly limit the supply and thus the availability of 
kerosene in relevant markets.  
In this context, prices of crude oil and crude oil-based 
products remain at a heightened level. This may have a 
significant adverse impact on fuel costs.  
Despite fuel hedging, an increase in revenue may only 
partly compensate for a higher fuel cost level or may do 
so subject to a time lag. 
Since kerosene is mainly purchased in US dollars, a 
higher fuel cost level may also increase the exchange 
rate sensitivity of the Company’s operating result. This 
risk cannot be ruled out entirely, despite the exchange 
rate hedges entered into.  
Geopolitical developments, the resulting higher cost 
and price levels, moderate global economic growth and 
prevailing geopolitical tensions may suppress demand 
and make it more difficult to forecast the volume of 
revenue.  
Risks also arise from overcapacities, economic 
fluctuations, global competition, possible changes in 
customer behaviour and other unpredictable 
geopolitical events.  
In the context of the ongoing integration of ITA 
Airways, potential impacts of this equity investment at 
Group level were considered for the first time in the risk 
report for the first quarter of 2026. The risk presented 
currently mainly relates to potential negative financial 
impacts of this equity investment on the Adjusted EBIT 
of Deutsche Lufthansa AG which deviate from the 
current planning. 
For the purpose of active risk mitigation, capacity and 
fleet measures are being brought forward and 
implemented in the short term. Possible additional 
flight cancellations and capacity restrictions cannot be 
ruled out. The Group plans to limit this risk potential by 
continuously monitoring the booking pattern and 
through flexible capacity planning. Sales, product and 
cost-cutting measures are also being implemented. 
Since 2005, the European Emissions Trading System 
(EU ETS) has constituted the European Union’s key 
climate change mitigation instrument. Inner-European 
air traffic is already covered by the EU ETS. On 
17 July 2026, the European Commission presented a 
proposal for a revised version of the EU ETS. Under this 
proposal, from 2029, the EU ETS will include not only all 
flights within the EU but also flights to airports within a 
5,000 km radius of Frankfurt Airport (the EU’s 
geographical centre). An expansion of the current EU 
ETS might lead to significant additional costs. In 
addition, the EU’s incentive mechanism is to be 
expanded and will thus reduce additional costs for 
sustainable fuels (SAF) on long-haul flights. However, 
whether and to what extent this will compensate for 
additional costs resulting from the expansion of the EU 
ETS will depend on how these reform proposals are 
actually implemented.  
There is a continuing risk of labour disputes as a result 
of pending collective bargaining agreements with 
various groups of employees within the Lufthansa 
Group. At the same time, the collective bargaining 
partners are currently seeking to return to a solution-
focused approach to negotiations.  
Ryanair has appealed to the General Court (of the 
European Court of Justice) against the decision by the 
European Commission approving stabilisation measures 
for companies in the Lufthansa Group. In May 2023, the 
European General Court upheld the action for 
annulment with regard to the stabilisation measure in 
the amount of EUR 6bn granted to Deutsche Lufthansa 
AG by the Economic Stabilisation Fund (ESF) of the 
Federal Republic of Germany and annulled the 
corresponding state aid decision of the European 
Commission on the grounds of substantive errors of 
law. Deutsche Lufthansa AG brought an appeal before 
the European Court of Justice against the lower court’s 
ruling. However, on 23 April 2026 the ECJ dismissed 
this appeal on revised grounds. Nonetheless, on the 
whole it followed Deutsche Lufthansa AG’s line of 
argument on key points and has revised much of the 
General Court’s ruling of May 2023. In July 2024, the 
European Commission already initiated a formal 
examination procedure relating to a re-approval, as it 
has done in similar cases. There is no immediate 
repayment risk as the stabilisation measures have 
already been completed and Deutsche Lufthansa AG 
has repaid the stabilisation funds it received in full.

Sector outlook 
In June, the International Air Transport Association (IATA) 
lowered its forecast for 2026 due to the effects of the crisis 
in the Middle East. Higher fuel costs, airspace disruptions 
and longer routes are having a negative impact on growth. 
However, the underlying demand for travel has not 
collapsed entirely and has merely weakened. 
The number of passenger-kilometres sold worldwide is now 
expected to grow by 2% in 2026, which represents a 
significant slowdown by comparison with the past few 
years (previous year: 5%). Growth of 5% had previously 
been predicted for 2026 as well. IATA’s forecast includes 
clear regional variations. Due to airspace restrictions, the 
Middle East faces a strong decline, while Africa and the 
Asia-Pacific region are set to grow thanks to the rerouting 
of air traffic. 
For the freight sector, IATA currently expects global 
revenue tonne-kilometres to increase by 1% in 2026 
(previous year: 3%). It had previously predicted a growth 
rate of 3%. 
Overall, IATA is forecasting a profit of USD 23.0bn in 2026 
(previous year: USD 45.0bn) for the global airline industry. 
It had previously predicted a profit of USD 41.0bn. 
Outlook for the Lufthansa 
Group 
Outlook subject to uncertainties 
The financial outlook for the Lufthansa Group is subject to 
a heightened level of forecasting uncertainty due to short-
term booking cycles in the passenger business, the fact 
that cargo business is largely driven by the spot market, the 
uncertain geopolitical and macroeconomic environment, 
the lack of clarity regarding the outcome of collective 
bargaining and the possibility of various professions 
striking, and the uncertainty regarding the exact delivery 
dates of new aircraft. 
In particular, the Group’s operational and financial 
performance is strongly influenced by the crisis in the 
Middle East. This is currently leading to operational 
disruptions such as the temporary cancellation of flight 
connections to the Gulf region. At the same time, demand 
on routes to Asia and Africa in particular has picked up 
significantly. This is likewise attributable to the situation in 
the Middle East – and to the hubs in the Gulf in particular – 
which is driving shifts in passenger flows.  
The significant increase in kerosene prices is putting 
considerable pressure on the Lufthansa Group passenger 
airlines’ cost position, even though around 86% of the 
volume of kerosene required for the current year is already 
covered by fuel hedging through derivatives on various oil 
products.  
Uncertainty in the macroeconomic outlook – in particular, 
trade policy tensions, scarce resources and supply 
bottlenecks, along with the effects of anti-inflationary 
measures implemented by the major central banks – may 
significantly impact customer demand.

The consolidated financial statements of Deutsche Lufthansa AG, Cologne, and its

In preparing the interim financial statements, the standards and interpretations valid as of

prepared using the same accounting policies as those on which the preceding consolidated 
financial statements as of 31 December 2025 were based. The standards and 
interpretations mandatory from 1 January 2026 onwards had no material effect on the 
Group’s net assets, financial and earnings position, and no restatements resulting from 
new standards were necessary.  
No significant changes to the group of consolidated companies occurred in the reporting 
period. However, in the second quarter of 2026 Deutsche Lufthansa AG exercised its

2. Matters of significance for the interim financial statements  
and going concern status 
In the first half of 2026, the Lufthansa Group’s business performance was substantially 
impacted by the effects of the crisis in the Middle East. The increase in the price of 
kerosene in particular adversely affected the Group’s operational and financial 
performance. Strong demand on Asian and African routes, by contrast, had a positive 
impact on traffic. The strikes staged by VC and UFO, moreover, had a negative impact.  
The Network Airlines and the Point-to-Point Airlines primarily increased their revenue due 
to higher yields and an increased passenger load factor. The above-mentioned issues 
adversely affected traffic. 
In the Logistics business segment, the positive operating and financial trend continued in 
the first half of 2026 due to increased yields and a higher sales volume. 
Growth and the earnings trend in the MRO business segment continued to be driven by 
strong demand for maintenance and repair services. In the second quarter, the geopolitical 
crises were reflected in reduced flight hours and a corresponding drop in demand for 
certain MRO services. The US dollar exchange rate trend, inflation- and growth-related 
cost increases and US tariffs had a negative effect. 
Operating activities generated a cash inflow of EUR 2,264m in the reporting period, 
primarily as a result of the positive change in trade working capital which is typical of this 
time of year. This was primarily due to cash inflows from ticket sales. In the first half of 
2026, the Lufthansa Group concluded the sale-and-lease-back of one short-haul aircraft 
and six long-haul aircraft. This is the key factor shaping the proceeds from the disposal of 
intangible assets, property, plant and equipment and other financial investments in the 
cash flow statement. 
As of 30 June 2026, Deutsche Lufthansa AG had centrally available liquidity of EUR 7.6bn 
(previous year: EUR 8.1bn). Decentralised bank balances and cash in hand came to a 
further EUR 0.5bn (previous year: EUR 0.5bn). Free credit lines of EUR 2.6bn are still 
available as of the reporting date (previous year: EUR 2.6bn). Altogether, the Lufthansa 
Group’s available liquidity therefore comes to EUR 10.7bn (previous year: EUR 11.1bn).  
Based on macroeconomic trends and expected customer behaviour, the Lufthansa Group 
regularly updates its profit and liquidity planning to reflect the changing parameters for its 
expected course of business. The international trade conflicts and the wars in the Middle 
East and Ukraine are the key factors currently causing uncertainty. Such geopolitical 
uncertainties and their economic consequences represent a material risk for the 
performance of the world economy, the entire aviation industry and the Lufthansa Group. 
These factors may be reflected in unfavourable supply situations on the procurement side 
as well as changes in demand on the sales side and related adverse price developments. In 
the context of the ongoing geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East, energy prices have 
risen considerably, including kerosene prices. Moreover, local supply bottlenecks cannot 
be ruled out, depending on how long the conflict continues. There are further uncertainties 
in connection with the public and political debate on climate protection.

Deferred taxes 
The same assessment criteria as before were applied for the assessment of the 
recoverability of deferred tax assets, in particular for loss carry-forwards. The losses 
incurred in recent years were due to an accumulation of exogenous factors (pandemic, 
supply and system partner bottlenecks, wars in Ukraine and the Middle East) whose 
simultaneous incidence was exceptional and is unlikely to be repeated over the next few 
years. This does not call into question the basic long-term profitability of the industry and, 
in particular, of the Deutsche Lufthansa AG tax group. Deutsche Lufthansa AG has in the 
past demonstrated its ability to achieve taxable profits over long-term periods. It therefore 
envisages continued taxable profits from 2027 and in subsequent years. While in Germany 
tax loss carry-forwards are not subject to any restrictions regarding the period of time in 
which they can be used, their use for tax purposes may nonetheless be excluded for other 
reasons. The uncertainty in this respect increases in line with the length of the planning 
period. Deferred tax assets on loss carry-forwards are therefore only recognised to the 
extent that they are actually expected to be used for tax purposes within ten years of the 
reporting date. 
In the reporting period, no further deferred tax assets on corporation tax loss carry-
forwards were capitalised for the tax losses accrued in the financial year to date for the tax 
group in Germany, the tax group in Austria and Brussels Airlines' companies.

4. Seasonality 
The Group’s business is exposed to seasonal effects in its Network Airlines, Point-to-Point 
Airlines and Logistics business segments. As such, passenger business revenue in the first 
and fourth quarters is generally lower, since people travel less, while higher revenue and 
operating earnings are normally generated in the second and third quarters. The Logistics 
segment tends to deliver its highest revenue and earnings contributions in the fourth 
quarter.

9. Segment reporting 
The structure of the segments was changed at the start of the 2026 financial year. As a 
result of the Group’s strategic realignment, which envisages deeper integration for the 
Network Airlines in terms of their organisational structure and processes, financial steering 
framework and collaboration, the Lufthansa Group’s Passenger Airlines segment has been 
divided up into the Network Airlines and Point-to-Point Airlines business segments. In 
future, the Network Airlines business segment will comprise Lufthansa Airlines, including 
Discover Airlines as well as the regional airlines Lufthansa CityLine, Lufthansa City Airlines 
and Air Dolomiti, SWISS together with Edelweiss, Austrian Airlines and Brussels Airlines. 
The equity investment in ITA Airways is also allocated to this segment. The Point-to-Point 
Airlines business segment includes Eurowings and the equity investment in SunExpress, a 
joint venture with Turkish Airlines. The figures for the previous year in the segment 
reporting have been adjusted accordingly.

11. Published standards that have not yet been applied 
The standard IFRS 18 “Presentation and Disclosure in Financial Statements” which was 
published during the 2024 financial year will mainly affect the structure of the income 
statement. The operating result reportable in future differs from the currently reported 
profit/loss from operating activities in terms of the definition of the items to be included in 
this subtotal. This is mainly due to the fact that, in future, expenses and income previously 
recognised within the profit/loss from operating activities will no longer be included in the 
operating result, while items currently reported within the scope of the financial result 
must in future be shown in the operating result. For instance, this relates to exchange 
gains and losses on liquidity holdings (no longer included in profit/loss from operating 
activities) or ineffective portions of fuel hedges, as well as interest income and expenses 
from income tax matters (in future, included in profit/loss from operating activities). The 
Group is currently reviewing the extent to which this will entail changes to the figures 
reported. Amendments of other accounting standards approved by the IASB as of the date 
of publication of this report which are applicable for financial years beginning after 
1 January 2026 did not have any material effect on the presentation of the net assets, 
financial and earnings position. Further information on the amendments resolved as of the 
date of preparation of the interim financial statements is provided in ↗ Note 2 to the 2025 
consolidated financial statements (Annual Report 2025, p. 261ff.)
